# Spectroscopy

MRI can be used not only to create images but also to measure the chemical composition of tissues.

This technique is called:

```text
Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy (MRS)
```

Instead of displaying anatomy, spectroscopy displays peaks that correspond to different chemical compounds.

---

# 3.13.1 ¹H Spectroscopy

¹H (Hydrogen) spectroscopy is the most common form of MR spectroscopy.

It measures metabolites containing hydrogen nuclei.

Common metabolites include:

- NAA (N-Acetyl Aspartate)
- Creatine (Cr)
- Choline (Cho)
- Lactate
- Lipids

Applications:

- Brain tumor evaluation
- Stroke assessment
- Metabolic disorders
- Neurodegenerative diseases

# 3.13.2 Multi-Nuclear Spectroscopy

MRI can also image nuclei other than hydrogen.

This is called:

```text
Multi-Nuclear Spectroscopy
```

Common nuclei include:

| Nucleus | Application |
|----------|-------------|
| ¹³C | Metabolic studies |
| ²³Na | Cell viability and sodium concentration |
| ³¹P | Energy metabolism (ATP, phosphocreatine) |
| ¹⁹F | Drug tracking and research |
| ¹⁷O | Oxygen metabolism research |

Applications:

- Research imaging
- Metabolic studies
- Cellular physiology
- Drug development
- Advanced tissue characterization

---

# Key Takeaways

- MR Spectroscopy (MRS) measures tissue chemistry rather than anatomy.
- ¹H spectroscopy is the most widely used spectroscopy technique in clinical MRI.
- Common metabolites measured include NAA, Creatine, Choline, Lactate, and Lipids.
- Spectroscopy displays frequency peaks rather than conventional images.
- Multi-nuclear spectroscopy uses nuclei such as ¹³C, ²³Na, ³¹P, and ¹⁹F.
- Multi-nuclear techniques are primarily used for research and advanced metabolic imaging.
