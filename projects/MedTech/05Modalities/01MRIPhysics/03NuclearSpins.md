# Nuclear Spins

The phenomenon of **Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR)** arises in certain atoms:

- Those with an odd number of protons and/or an odd number of neutrons.
- Examples include:
  - ¹H
  - ¹³C
  - ²³Na

These atoms possess a spin angular momentum vector: **S**

There is a magnetic dipole moment associated with this spin:
**μ = γS**
where:
- **γ** = gyromagnetic ratio
- **S** = spin angular momentum vector

---

## Additional Notes

- γ is unique for different nuclei.
- h is Planck's constant.
- I is the quantum mechanical spin operator.
- In the human body, which is mostly water, **¹H nuclei** are by far the most abundant and most commonly used in MRI.

### Gyromagnetic Ratio of Hydrogen

**γ = 42.57638507 MHz/T**

### Classical Interpretation

From a classical physics viewpoint, each nucleus (often referred to as a *spin*) behaves like a charged sphere rotating on its axis.

This rotation creates:
1. A current loop
2. A magnetic dipole moment

To understand an MR signal, we must understand how these spins interact with:
- The static magnetic field (**B₀**)
- The RF magnetic field (**B₁**)
